# Idea 5: Glow Markers + UV Flash — Cons

## Critical Problems

### 🔴 UV LED Range is Very Short
- Cheap UV LEDs (the kind in ~50 INR blacklight keychains) only effectively illuminate fluorescent material at **0.5-1.5 meters**.
- Laser tag requires engagement distances of 5-15 meters. UV LEDs simply can't reach that far.
- **Powerful UV flashlights** that work at range cost 500-1000+ INR each, blowing the budget immediately.

### 🔴 UV Safety Concerns
- UV-A LEDs (365-400nm) can cause eye damage with direct or prolonged exposure.
- In a dark room where pupils are fully dilated, even brief UV exposure is more dangerous.
- Players accidentally shining UV guns in each other's eyes is almost guaranteed.
- **Liability risk is serious**, especially with younger players.

### 🟡 Marker Reading Still Needs Light
- Even if the fluorescent marker glows, the phone camera needs to capture AND decode it (read the ArUco pattern). A faint, brief glow may not produce a clean enough image.
- We're back to the same motion blur and processing problems as Idea 1, just with worse lighting.

### 🟡 Fluorescent Paint Cost
- Quality fluorescent/glow paint that reacts well to UV is not as cheap as regular paint. Getting enough for 12 sets of markers adds up.

### 🟢 Minor Issues
- **Glow persistence:** Some glow materials stay lit for seconds after UV exposure, which could cause confusion about when a hit actually happened.
- **Marker durability:** Glow paint on fabric or paper can crack, peel, or smudge during physical gameplay.

## Verdict
**UV range and safety are dealbreakers.** The concept is visually amazing but physically impractical at the distances and budget required. Would be great for a higher-budget, controlled commercial setup with proper safety gear.
