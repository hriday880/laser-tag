# Idea 11: Hybrid Overwatch (Dual Phone) — Cons

## Problems

### 🔴 Requires 24 Phones for 12 Players
- This is the single biggest barrier. Not everyone has a spare old phone lying around.
- Even if they do, old phones might be too slow, have dead batteries, or broken screens.
- You can't guarantee this for a casual group of 12 people.

### 🟡 App Must Run on Diverse Old Phones
- The shield phone app needs to run on potentially ancient Android 5/6 devices with tiny RAM.
- Building and testing across such a wide range of devices is a significant development burden.

### 🟡 Complexity Doubles
- Two apps to build (gun app + shield app), two devices to pair, two batteries to keep charged, two devices to troubleshoot per player.
- Setup time for 12 players × 2 devices = chaos.

### 🟡 Weight and Comfort
- Old phones are often heavier than modern ones. Strapping a heavy old phone to your chest adds fatigue.

### 🟢 Minor Issues
- **Screen burn-in:** Displaying a static bright pattern for extended periods can cause OLED burn-in on some old phones.
- **WiFi bandwidth:** 24 devices on one router is pushing it for a cheap home router. Need a decent dual-band router.
- **Bluetooth pairing:** If using Bluetooth triggers, managing 12 paired triggers across 24 phones gets messy.

## Verdict
**The 24-phone requirement is a soft dealbreaker for a casual group.** If you happen to have a group where everyone has an old spare phone, this is the richest gameplay experience possible. But it's not realistic to guarantee for most groups.

**Best used as an optional "premium mode"** — for players who have two phones, offer the dual-phone experience. For others, fall back to Idea 9's LED beacons.
